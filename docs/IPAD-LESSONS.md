# iPad lesson design: drawing before reading

**Design rule:** It should feel like looking over someone’s shoulder while they draw, not opening a dashboard. A child who never reads a button name should still be able to start, follow a mark, replay it, and get unstuck. Keep text and accessible names for the people and tools that need them, but do not use them as the only cue. Optimize the drawing view for iPad in portrait and landscape first.

## Ranked original changes to try in Little fox

1. **Show the move, not more controls.** Make the current example the biggest element. Fade prior lines just enough to make the new stroke stand out; one visible move at a time, then hold the finished example until the child advances. The existing Paper mode already animates strokes and offers Replay; aim for more legible emphasis, not a new video system.
2. **One obvious next action, one safe way back.** Use a prominent Next cue with a familiar shape/icon plus a label; keep large Replay and Back controls visibly distinct. Put an optional start dot or short directional ghost on a tricky stroke only; reduce motion to a still with the same start/end cue. Keep the pencil, navigation and instruction visible together on common iPad widths, without getting covered by overlays or menus.
3. **Vary help without making kids choose before drawing.** Easy can show one wide mark and a starting point. Medium can combine two marks and invite a choice. Hard can hide the model and ask the learner to apply the same idea to something different. None is tied to age or makes copying perfectly the goal. One action-sized prompt and optional contextual help beat a tour or paragraph.
4. **Observe before expanding.** With caregiver permission, try the first lesson in portrait and landscape on an actual iPad with learners at both ends of the age range. Without adult coaching or reading labels aloud, can the learner identify the next tap/mark, replay after missing it, recover from Back, and continue? Ask them to show the move on a different drawing. Record anonymous counts only and revise any step that consistently requires adult explanation. Keep a paper fallback and verify reduced motion with the example still legible.

Do not add a results dashboard, levels screen before play, points, ticker, tutorial overlay, or audio requirement. Reuse the existing original art paths and browser-local game controls.
