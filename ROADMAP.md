# Little Pencil Club roadmap

Little Pencil Club is a paper-first drawing game for **ages 5–10**. Build for a child to start on their own, learn a move they can reuse, and end with a drawing that feels like theirs. The order below is a priority list, not a release promise.

**iPad-first design:** Drawing fills the screen; each step has one obvious next action, large touch targets, and a way to replay or recover. A child should not have to read button labels or notes to get started. No dashboard or tour. See the [iPad lesson design and Simply Draw visual references](docs/IPAD-LESSONS.md).

## Already shipped

- [x] Paper-first Back, Replay, and Next; optional in-lesson trace and blank canvas.
- [x] 32 original lessons across six topics, with all three numeric difficulty levels represented.
- [x] Local Sketchbook, digital PNG export, and post-lesson remix prompts.
- [x] Printable Pencil Pal cards.

## Unified ranked backlog

**Partial** means a relevant foundation exists; **missing** means the proposed capability was not found in the current repo; **verify** means current coverage needs a focused check. Checked means shipped, not merely scoped. Ranked for first-mark speed on school iPads, useful drawing practice for ages 5–10, dependencies, and privacy. Ranks are not issue IDs. An independent learning/product review and a separate rollout/security review informed this order. Start with public QR → first pencil mark → reusable drawing skill. Keep the D1 classroom proof separate, with synthetic data only. No camera, uploads, AI, class membership, paywall, app install, mandatory walkthrough or adult toggle in the first pilot.

### First school QR pilot

1. [ ] **[Partial] Make the school handout work on iPad.** Print one existing Pencil Pal QR card at actual size and test on a school-managed iPad on its normal network where available; try the typed URL fallback too. Done when the public code opens the game and a child can start drawing without an account or a mandatory walkthrough. Put tiny optional grown-up help for lining up/scanning the QR, capturing a paper drawing, and using a separate private class invite only where relevant. Verify the paper finish message distinguishes saved lesson progress from the physical drawing. Digital PNG readback in Files is a separate smoke test, not required to make the first paper mark. Keep a one-page black-and-white pencil exercise as a fallback if the network blocks the game. A public handout must never grant access to a private gallery. [Issue #4](https://github.com/Shane-Neeley/little-pencil-club/issues/4)
2. [ ] **[Partial] Teach one reusable skill in Little fox.** Show light and dark marks side by side, pause for a scrap-paper try, then use the marks on the fox’s tail. Let the child choose fluffy, striped, or dragon-scorched, and invite them to use the same trick on a different subject without a trace. **Done when:** the entire loop works on paper with Replay, Next, and Skip, and the child can point to the choice they made. [Issue #1](https://github.com/Shane-Neeley/little-pencil-club/issues/1)
3. [ ] **[Partial] Make lessons usable with little reading.** Add clear action icons, one action per prompt, and large touch targets; use a paper fallback if the QR cannot open. Optional audio can wait for evidence. Done when an early reader can follow a lesson and mute or replay support without leaving it.
4. [ ] **[Partial] Make Easy / Medium / Hard clear and self-selected.** Keep the existing three pencil marks and numeric difficulty data; make all three names findable and consistent (the hardest is currently labeled Challenge). Define **Easy** as one big, clearly demonstrated action at a time; **Medium** as combining two moves with a creative choice; **Hard** as an optional independent challenge with less scaffolding. **Done when:** the library signals difficulty in text as well as with icons, and the fox starts immediately with no level picker required, and a child can later pick or skip a challenge without an age gate, sign-in, or locked core lesson. [Issue #2](https://github.com/Shane-Neeley/little-pencil-club/issues/2)
5. [ ] **[Missing] Test the fox lesson before expanding the library.** Try the current replay and a skill-first paper prototype with willing learners across the age range, with caregiver permission. Observe scan-to-first-mark on a managed iPad, portrait and landscape, without reading labels aloud or coaching. Check independent start, replay and recovery, cues needed, and whether the child uses the skill on an unmodeled subject. **Done when:** anonymous observations support a documented iterate-or-expand decision. Expand only if most learners in the small pilot can reuse the skill unprompted and younger learners can start on their own. Do not collect names, photos, or recordings. [Issue #3](https://github.com/Shane-Neeley/little-pencil-club/issues/3)

### Next, if the first pilot is useful

6. [ ] **[Missing] Save a paper drawing from iPad Safari.** Prototype the browser photo picker and, where supported, camera capture. Show a preview, let the learner crop the paper, and provide retake, cancel, local save, PNG export, and delete. Done when capture and picker fallback are verified on a real iPad and denial or accidental cancellation leaves drawing intact. No network upload.
7. [ ] **[Missing] Make a paper drawing wiggle.** Take the cropped drawing, preserve the original, and preview an on-device bounce, wiggle, or wobble. Done when the child can pick a motion, replay it, stop it, and keep the still drawing without any image leaving the device. Evaluate smarter local cutout or motion effects only after the simple version is fun; off-device AI is a separate decision.
8. [ ] **[Partial] Link lessons by skill, not just subject.** Start with light/dark and looking closely at shapes or textures. For the latter, try an everyday object or leaf: look, draw one part, look again. **Done when:** at least two skills each have a short demo, a guided subject, and another subject that uses the same skill with less help.
9. [ ] **[Partial] Try one optional Sketchbook reflection.** Ask a child to point to or say what they changed, with a skip and no score. **Done when:** the prompt works for paper drawers and does not require typing, an upload, or a network account. Defer studio decoration, currencies, streaks, and automated critiques until there’s a clear reason to build them.
10. [ ] **[Verify] Do an accessibility and input pass.** Cover large touch targets, non-color level indicators, focus and keyboard navigation, contrast, reduced motion, and pen/finger/mouse options. Done when the first lesson is tested on touch and keyboard and supports Apple Pencil without making it a requirement.
11. [ ] **[Partial] Make portable paper activities.** Build on the printable Pencil Pal cards with a printable skill mini-lesson or reference sheet. Done when it can be printed in black and white and finished with ordinary pencil and paper, without a connection.
12. [ ] **[Verify] Add a focused cross-device QA checklist.** Verify Safari/iPad with pen and finger, Android touch, and desktop; test resume, redraw, export and Back. Done when a repeatable release check exists; do not promise device support without evidence.
13. [ ] **[Partial] Make the first drawing obvious.** Keep the existing no-account immediate play with no mandatory tour. Use contextual, skippable hints while drawing; test whether paper and screen choices need a clearer cue rather than adding another screen. Done when a new learner reaches the first mark without an account or intake form.
14. [ ] **[Partial] Add original reference views.** Show an actual object or rights-cleared reference next to, not over, the child’s drawing; contrast it with the simplified example. Done when a child can hide the model for independent practice and switch it back on.
15. [ ] **[Partial] Add more ways to learn at your own pace.** Preserve Back/Replay/Next, existing resume and reduced-motion handling; consider adjustable speed or a still-step option. Done when pausing, going back, or reloading does not lose the learner’s place or work.
16. [ ] **[Partial] Add a repeat-and-revisit path.** Revisit a familiar skill in a later session with a new subject instead of replaying the identical answer. Done when at least one skill can be retried and revisited without a streak, deadline, or push notification.
17. [ ] **[Missing] Offer a “what should I draw next?” suggestion.** Use a locally chosen topic, level, or practiced skill; keep all paths open and switchable. Done when recommendations can be changed or reset with no profile, date of birth, or tracking.
18. [ ] **[Partial] Make a free-draw Playground.** Surface independent drawing and remix prompts without requiring lesson completion. Done when children can start blank, grab or skip an idea, and keep their own drawing. Keep existing trace and paper modes genuinely optional.
19. [ ] **[Partial] Set up an original lesson authoring checklist.** Build on the existing lesson data. Done when a new lesson is checked for valid paths/steps, alternate prompts, level, estimated time, reference rights, and a usable transfer exercise; changes to a demo update related modes consistently.
20. [ ] **[Partial] Make progress and saves resilient.** Keep local Sketchbook and PNG export; consider local restore/export and offline play as a web app. Done when offline restart, partial saves, storage limits, and clearing site data have tested, understandable outcomes.

### Parallel work, not a pilot dependency

21. [ ] **[Missing / feasibility first] Build and verify a synthetic-only private classroom gallery.** A separate expiring, revocable private QR opens the class for kids without accounts, an adult toggle, or an approval queue. Only automatically screened drawings would be shown; operator removal and invite revocation stay behind the scenes. Test teacher-managed access, automatic drawing-only screening before save or sharing (with no per-drawing adult approval), report and removal controls, and an access model that prevents guessing links or crossing between classes. Avoid names, faces, public child profiles, comments and public feeds. Done at this stage when a fake-data prototype and adult/school review establish access, consent, retention and removal before any real sharing is enabled. Evaluate D1 for class membership, screening state and expiry metadata and a separate private image store for files; do not assume storage, authentication or hosting access is available.

### Later, only if needed

22. [ ] **[Missing] Explore a caregiver or classroom guide.** Give adults simple non-grading prompts and short printable activities, not a student monitoring dashboard. Done when one printable session works without child accounts or sending drawings anywhere.
23. [ ] **[Missing] Try a same-room Draw Together activity.** Take turns advancing one shared paper-first prompt while each child draws separately. Done when solo play still works and two children can keep separate art without cloud accounts or sharing.
24. [ ] **[Partial] Build private progress around what was tried.** Use the existing local progress to highlight skills explored, favorites or before/after work. Done when kids can celebrate a decision or practice attempt without a score, leaderboard, or locked essentials.
25. [ ] **[Missing] Add a Pencil Pal story or seasonal studio prompt.** Prototype one short, original seasonal drawing quest or character story inspired by the idea of themed extras, not a copy. Done when it can be played independently and does not require a date, streak or personal info.
26. [ ] **[Missing] Try optional short teacher-style demos.** Try one original hand-and-pencil clip with captions and a pause, comparing it with the silent replay. If narration helps, explore a short, explicitly approved synthetic narration in the project owner’s own voice; keep audio optional, label the narration, and retain captions and the silent replay. Keep voice references and credentials out of the repository. Done when it earns its additional weight by making a skill easier to start or understand.
27. [ ] **[Missing] Try optional studio decoration and artwork animations.** Start with one locally earned or freely selected room decoration or pencil animation. Done when it’s optional, respects reduced motion, and does not delay or gate drawing.
28. [ ] **[Partial] Explore more original paths and skill range.** Put cartoon expressions, environments, clothing, short comics, perspective, layering and composition behind demand and the learning-pilot results. Done per path when there is clear scaffolding, independent practice, and original art.
29. [ ] **[Missing] Explore localization.** Make the first lesson and navigation available in a second language, with independently selectable narration if added. Done when language changes do not reset progress and copy/captions have been checked by a fluent reader.
30. [ ] **[Missing] Explore separate local sketchbooks for siblings.** Keep names optional and do not collect ages. Done when a caregiver can move between and remove local sketchbooks without mixing work. Cloud accounts or cross-device sync are a separate, deferred decision.
31. [ ] **[Missing / defer] Consider cross-device progress and Journal backup.** Test local export/restore first; require a separate minimization and deletion design before any cloud sync. No child account required for core play.
32. [ ] **[Missing / defer] Explore an on-device drawing replay export.** If drawing-process export proves useful, use locally saved strokes and an explicit export action, not camera or wearable capture. No hosted gallery, recipient comments, or automated capture planned.

### Not needed for this pilot

33. [ ] **[Missing] Explore optional Sparks-like progression.** If basic progress is not enough, try optional cosmetic rewards for variety or practice, not attendance or accuracy. Done when a comparison shows it helps children return to drawing without frustration, pressure, or gating core lessons.
34. [ ] **[Missing / defer] Consider native apps and adult-only subscriptions.** Web first. Revisit app stores, account recovery, restore purchases, and pricing only after demand is clear. Done if pursued when play and payment are separate, guardian controls are explicit, and the paid rules are tested. No near-term monetization decision.
35. [ ] **[Missing / defer] Consider optional human encouragement before automated critique.** Begin with a local caregiver prompt. Any off-device teacher workflow would require a separate child-privacy, consent, deletion and moderation design before development. No default uploads, open chat or public gallery.
36. [ ] **[Not planned] Automated feedback on artwork.** Simply’s [teacher FAQ](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/17676294-who-is-tom-is-he-real) describes human-written replies, while its privacy notice also describes an AI-powered artwork-feedback feature named Tom, including video responses and voice suggestions. The relationship between those flows and availability of instant feedback are unverified. We will not score children’s artwork or upload it for machine critique under this roadmap; safety screening is a separate feature.

## Optional intelligence

See the [drawing screening, animation and bring-your-own-provider plan](docs/INTELLIGENCE.md). Core drawing and motion effects remain local-first. Class photo sharing stays off until the automatic screening and privacy gates are verified; no cloud AI is enabled by this plan.

## Evidence and scope

- Simply Draw documents [onboarding and the starter fish](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/10258428-how-soon-will-i-get-to-draw-something), [paths, skill lessons, Playground and photo Journal](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/10260104-what-is-simply-draw), [paper-only drawing](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/9256539-simply-draw-most-common-faqs), [Playground levels and Sparks](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/15859831-playground-levels-sparks), [Studio](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/14636876-what-is-my-studio), [Tom’s Stories](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/15055217-what-is-tom-s-stories), [optional teacher messages](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/15061680-send-my-drawings-to-the-teacher), [language controls](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/12150988-changing-the-app-language-in-simply-draw), [family profiles](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/12151347-add-delete-or-reset-a-profile), and [subscriptions](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/9256419-subscribe-to-simply-draw). Supplemental materials document [one-skill practice minis](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/15046234-tips-tricks-playground) and [reference observation and reusable techniques](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/10258504-how-does-simply-draw-help-me-to-become-an-independent-artist). Additional documented experiences include [Draw Together](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/16297111-draw-together-ios-only), [multi-device login](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/11111942-log-in-on-multiple-devices), its [privacy description of Tom and sharing](https://www.hellosimply.com/legal/privacy), and a [wearable capture integration](https://www.hellosimply.com/ai-glasses-from-meta). Observational reference use is documented; the specific photo UI and video-pause behavior are not confirmed across versions. This is a public-documentation inventory, not an exhaustive hands-on audit; platform, language and account eligibility vary. An existing difference worth preserving: Little Pencil Club already supports paper, trace, and blank digital drawing, with browser-local saves and no sign-in.
- [Vlach & Carver: observation coaching in kindergarten](https://ecrp.illinois.edu/v10n1/vlach.html). In one class of 22, explicit prompts to check a model before, during, and after drawing improved observation and the detail of drawings. This supports trying a concrete look-again cue; it does not establish app outcomes or make realism the goal.
- [Bremner et al.: cube drawing among 7–10-year-olds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10953929/). In one experiment, tracing did not improve subsequent independent cube drawing; highlighting faces rather than edges helped. That narrow task supports optional tracing and an untraced check, not a blanket claim against tracing.
- [California Visual Arts standards](https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/arts?c0=5&c3=3,9,5). Early grades include describing the process and explaining choices. This informs optional show-and-tell, not a quiz or an art grade. Difficulty routes and transfer tests here are product hypotheses, not educational benchmarks.
