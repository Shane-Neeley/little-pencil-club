# Little Pencil Club roadmap

Little Pencil Club is a paper-first drawing game for **ages 5–10**. Build for a child to start on their own, learn a move they can reuse, and end with a drawing that feels like theirs. The order below is a priority list, not a release promise.

## Ranked checklist

1. [ ] **Teach one reusable skill in Little fox.** Show light and dark marks side by side, pause for a scrap-paper try, then use the marks on the fox’s tail. Let the child choose fluffy, striped, or dragon-scorched, and invite them to use the same trick on a different subject without a trace. **Done when:** the entire loop works on paper with Replay, Next, and Skip, and the child can point to the choice they made.
2. [ ] **Make Easy / Medium / Hard clear and self-selected.** Keep the existing three pencil marks and numeric difficulty data; make all three names findable and consistent (the hardest is currently labeled Challenge). Define **Easy** as one big, clearly demonstrated action at a time; **Medium** as combining two moves with a creative choice; **Hard** as an optional independent challenge with less scaffolding. **Done when:** the library signals difficulty in text as well as with icons, and a child can pick or skip a challenge without an age gate, sign-in, or locked core lesson.
3. [ ] **Test the fox lesson before expanding the library.** Try the current replay and a skill-first paper prototype with willing learners across the age range, with caregiver permission. Observe independent start, cues needed, and whether the child uses the skill on an unmodeled subject. **Done when:** anonymous observations support a documented iterate-or-expand decision. Expand only if most learners in the small pilot can reuse the skill unprompted and younger learners can start on their own. Do not collect names, photos, or recordings.
4. [ ] **Link lessons by skill, not just subject.** Start with light/dark and looking closely at shapes or textures. For the latter, try an everyday object or leaf: look, draw one part, look again. **Done when:** at least two skills each have a short demo, a guided subject, and another subject that uses the same skill with less help.
5. [ ] **Try one optional Sketchbook reflection.** Ask a child to point to or say what they changed, with a skip and no score. **Done when:** the prompt works for paper drawers and does not require typing, an upload, or a network account. Defer studio decoration, currencies, streaks, and automated critiques until there’s a clear reason to build them.

## Simply Draw comparison

| Area | Simply Draw | Little Pencil Club / opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawing surface and pace | Pencil and paper with teacher-led video | Paper first with silent stroke replays at the child’s pace; optional trace and blank canvas. Keep the controls and make the pause an invitation to draw. |
| Skill progression | Skill lesson, guided subject, then fresh practice | 32 original lessons already have skill labels, optional tips, and remixes. Add a brief warm-up and test the skill on a new subject. |
| Reflection and rewards | Photo Journal; Sparks unlock a Studio on eligible iPads | Local Sketchbook and PNG export for digital work. Try optional reflection before adding rewards or photo collection. |

## Research behind the priorities

- [Vlach & Carver: observation coaching in kindergarten](https://ecrp.illinois.edu/v10n1/vlach.html). In one class of 22, explicit prompts to look before, during, and after drawing increased checking the model and improved detail and accuracy. This supports testing a concrete look-again cue; it does not establish app outcomes or make realism the goal.
- [California Visual Arts standards](https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/arts?c0=5&c3=3,9,5). Early grades include describing the process and explaining choices. This informs optional show-and-tell, not a quiz or an art grade.
- Simply Draw’s official [learning paths and Journal](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/10260104-what-is-simply-draw), [starter fish](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/10258428-how-soon-will-i-get-to-draw-something), and [Studio](https://draw-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/14636876-what-is-my-studio) document the comparison, not educational effectiveness.

When shipping an item, check it off in the same commit or PR as the work and add a link or a short verification note. Keep new artwork and lesson writing original.
